[0:01.2] Hook
This support bot has been rewritten five times, mostly by an AI coach. Every new version had to beat the champion in a live Flagsmith experiment, and a person approves every change.

[0:13.4] The problem
Today, teams change the prompts behind their AI features on gut feel. Tweak the prompt, read a few answers, deploy, and hope. Nobody measures what customers actually experience.

[0:25.4] The idea: one flag holds the agent's brain
Darwin puts the agent's whole brain, its prompt, model and effort, into one Flagsmith multivariate flag. The champion is the control, and every challenger is a variant. So changing the agent is a flag change, with no redeploy.

[0:41.3] Generation 1 fails
Our first version handed a quarter of its chats to humans. Here, it fixes a late order, then offers a specialist anyway. The customer says yes, and a human gets a ticket that was already solved.

[0:53.9] The Coach writes a challenger
Darwin's Coach, built on Claude Opus, read twenty-eight failed chats. It found the patterns, with counts and real quotes, and wrote a challenger with a hypothesis that Flagsmith can test.

[1:06.2] A person approves
Nothing goes live until a person approves. Every card shows the evidence, the prompt diff, and the exact Flagsmith CLI commands it will run, in the approver's name.

[1:18.0] Flagsmith decides
Flagsmith splits the traffic fifty-fifty, across a hundred and sixty simulated customers, and its Bayesian engine scores both versions. The challenger resolved ninety-one percent of chats, against sixty-nine, with a ninety-nine point eight percent chance to win. One more approval, and Flagsmith rolls it out to everyone. No deploy.

[1:38.8] More generations
Then the Coach cut cost by eight percent. A teammate's two-line prompt cut another twenty-seven, but it had dropped the escalation rules. So the Coach put back only what mattered: one hundred percent resolved.

[1:51.9] The guardrail (live run)
In a live run, a teammate tried skipping policy lookups to save money, and Anugrah approved the test. Flagsmith, scoring cost alone, gave it a ninety-four percent chance to win. But resolution collapsed, from ninety-nine percent to forty-five. Darwin's guardrail recommended a kill, Anugrah approved it, and every customer stayed on the champion.
